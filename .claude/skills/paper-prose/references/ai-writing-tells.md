# AI writing tells — distilled guide (2026-10)

Source: Wikipedia, "Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing

Version note: this first version was distilled from the page as known through mid-2026; Wikipedia could not be fetched live when it was built. The monthly refresh replaces it with a version checked against the live page.

## How to use this guide

- The page is descriptive, not a rulebook. One sign proves nothing; density is what reads as machine-written. Three "pivotal"s, a "not X but Y" and a closing "In summary" in one page is the problem, not any single item.
- The signs point at deeper problems: vague claims, puffery, filler, and statements nobody sourced. Fix those. Swapping a flagged word for a synonym leaves the problem and usually creates a new tell.
- Default fix: replace the generic claim with a specific fact, number, name or example, or cut it.
- Some signs are Wikipedia-specific (markup, citation quirks, curly quotes). They are listed last and matter little outside Wikipedia.

## 1. Content and framing

### Inflated significance and legacy
Claims that something is important, symbolic or lasting, with no evidence attached.
- Phrases: stands as / serves as a testament, plays a vital / pivotal / crucial role, underscores its importance, marks a key turning point, a watershed moment, leaves a lasting impact, enduring legacy, deeply rooted, continues to captivate / inspire, solidifies its place, reflects broader trends, setting the stage for.
- Fix: state what happened and let the reader judge importance. If significance matters, cite who says so and why.

### Overstated notability and media name-dropping
Listing outlets, awards, features or "recognition" to make the subject seem important ("has been featured in Forbes, TechCrunch and…").
- Fix: keep only coverage that carries a specific fact.

### Promotional, travel-brochure tone
- Words: vibrant, rich (heritage, history, tapestry), breathtaking, stunning, nestled, in the heart of, must-visit, boasts, renowned, groundbreaking, innovative, cutting-edge, world-class, seamless, unparalleled, commitment to excellence.
- Fix: neutral description; concrete features instead of adjectives.

### Editorializing and signposting
- Phrases: it's important to note, it is worth noting, notably, it should be noted, no discussion of X would be complete without, interestingly, crucially.
- Fix: delete the signpost and state the point.

### Superficial analysis via trailing -ing clauses
A sentence ends with a participle phrase that asserts meaning without support: "…, highlighting its importance", "…, emphasizing the need for", "…, reflecting a broader commitment to", "…, contributing to the region's growth", "…, ensuring that…", "…, fostering a sense of community".
- Fix: drop the clause, or turn it into a separate sourced claim.

### Vague attributions (weasel wording)
- Phrases: experts say, critics argue, observers have noted, industry reports suggest, many believe, some have described, has been described as.
- Fix: name the person, paper or organization, or remove the claim.

### Formulaic "challenges and future prospects" endings
"Despite its successes, X faces several challenges, including… Looking ahead, X is poised to…", "The future of X remains promising."
- Fix: cut, or replace with specific open problems.

### Section and paragraph summaries
- Phrases: In summary, In conclusion, Overall, To sum up, Ultimately, In essence, restating the paragraph in its last sentence.
- Fix: end on the last substantive point.

## 2. Sentence-level patterns

### Negative parallelism and contrast framing
- "It's not X, it's Y." "Not X, but Y." "Not only X, but also Y." "This isn't just about X — it's about Y." "No X, no Y, just Z."
- Setting up a claim nobody made so it can be knocked down.
- Fix: state Y directly.

### Rule of three
Reflexive triplets of adjectives, nouns or clauses ("innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking"; "speed, scale, and simplicity").
- Fix: keep the one or two items that carry weight; use three only when there are actually three things.

### Copula avoidance
Using "serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "represents", "marks" where "is" works; "boasts", "features", "offers" where "has" works.
- Fix: use is / has.

### Elegant variation (synonym cycling)
Rotating names for the same thing to avoid repetition ("the city… the metropolis… the urban center"; "the model… the system… the architecture").
- Fix: pick one term and repeat it. Repetition is clearer.

### False ranges
"From X to Y" where X and Y are not ends of any scale ("from ancient rituals to modern festivals", "from startups to governments").
- Fix: list the actual items or name the category.

### Transition overuse
Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition, Consequently, On the other hand, Indeed, starting many sentences.
- Fix: delete most; let sentence order carry the logic.

### Hedging stacks and filler
"It could be argued that", "in many ways", "to some extent", "plays a role in", "a wide range of", "various", "a variety of", "in today's fast-paced world", "in the ever-evolving landscape of".
- Fix: commit to the claim or drop it; replace "various" with the actual items.

## 3. AI vocabulary

Individually harmless; clustered, a strong tell. The list shifts as models change, so treat it as a frequency signal, not a ban list.

delve, tapestry, testament, intricate / intricacies, pivotal, underscore, highlight (verb), landscape (abstract), realm, foster, enhance, bolster, garner, showcase, crucial, vital, key (adjective), valuable, vibrant, meticulous / meticulously, nuanced, multifaceted, robust, seamless, comprehensive, profound, interplay, align with, resonate, navigate (abstract), embark, journey (abstract), commitment to, ever-evolving, game-changer, leverage (verb), unlock, empower, elevate, harness, paradigm, synergy, holistic, transformative, cutting-edge, Additionally (sentence-initial), enduring, notably.

- Fix: use the plain word (use, show, help, important, big, careful, detailed) or a specific fact.

## 4. Formatting

- Excessive boldface: bolding key phrases throughout running prose.
- Inline-header bullets: every bullet starting with "**Term:** explanation", even when prose would read better.
- Lists and headings for content that is a short paragraph; tables for two or three items.
- Title Case Headings in contexts that use sentence case.
- Emoji as bullet markers or section decoration in otherwise formal text.
- Em-dash overuse, especially paired em dashes around asides and "X — and that matters." One per page is fine; one per paragraph reads as AI.
- Markdown syntax (##, **, ```) pasted into places that don't render it.
- Fix: prose by default; formatting only where the reader scans or compares.

## 5. Chatbot residue

- Collaborative / assistant phrasing left in the output: Certainly!, Of course!, Great question, I hope this helps, Let me know if you'd like…, Would you like me to…, Here is a…, Feel free to…
- Knowledge-cutoff and gap disclaimers: as of my last update, as of my knowledge cutoff, while specific details are limited / not widely available, further research would be needed.
- Refusal or AI self-reference: As an AI language model…
- Placeholders left unfilled: [Your Name], [Company], INSERT_DATE, [citation needed] invented by the writer.
- Sycophancy toward the reader or the subject.
- Fix: delete. Fill or remove placeholders.

## 6. Wikipedia-specific signs (low relevance elsewhere)

- Broken or mixed wikitext and Markdown; citation artifacts such as utm_source=chatgpt.com, oaicite / contentReference markers, turn0search0-style tokens.
- Fabricated or mismatched references: dead links, wrong DOIs/ISBNs, real sources that don't support the sentence. Outside Wikipedia this still matters: verify every citation and link.
- Curly quotes and apostrophes mixed with straight ones (a weak sign; many editors produce them).
- Skipped heading levels, odd capitalization of section names, "External links" lists of irrelevant sites.

## 7. Caveats from the page

- Human writers, especially in marketing, academic and corporate registers, use many of these patterns. The guide catalogs what is common in model output, not what is wrong.
- AI-detection tools are unreliable; the page advises human judgment over detectors.
- Polished model text can have none of the surface signs and still carry the deeper problems (unsupported claims, fabricated sources). Check substance, not just style.
