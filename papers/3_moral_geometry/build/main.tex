\begin{abstract}
How do language models organize moral knowledge? Models detect moral
content broadly, but detection is a low bar. We ask whether they go
further: distinguishing moral foundations from one another and
organizing the relationships between them.

Training six independent linear probes on OLMo-2~1B and 7B and
OLMoE-1B-7B, one per foundation in Moral Foundations Theory (MFT),
shows integration. Foundation directions in representation space are
geometrically distinct, spanning five effective dimensions, yet share
a common moral-salience component. This is genuine multi-dimensional
structure, not a single moral detector. The geometry is consistent
across scale (1B to 7B dense), across dense and MoE architectures, and
on the independently constructed Moral Foundations Vignettes, and it
stabilizes early in pretraining.

The structure the model develops is not the structure moral psychology
predicts. Neither hierarchical clustering of the probe directions nor
unsupervised clustering of the activations recovers the
individualizing/binding distinction central to MFT; the model's moral
taxonomy is grounded in corpus statistics rather than human theoretical
categories. Per-foundation fragility testing shows a further
asymmetry: sanctity/degradation is the most robust foundation in both
dense models but the most fragile in MoE, far exceeding the overall
architectural gap.

Extending to moral dilemmas shows partial compositionality: dilemma
representations share significant variance with their component
foundations but encode largely conflict-specific structure beyond them,
with balanced loading suggesting the model represents moral conflict
itself rather than a pre-resolved judgment.
\end{abstract}
