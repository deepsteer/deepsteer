\begin{abstract}
How do large language models (LLMs) organize moral knowledge? Models
detect moral content broadly, but detection is a low bar. We ask
whether they go further, distinguishing moral foundations from one
another and organizing the relationships between them geometrically.

We train six independent linear probes on open-weight language models
(OLMo-2~1B and 7B, OLMoE-1B-7B), one per Moral Foundations Theory (MFT)
category (\emph{care/harm}, \emph{fair/cheat}, \emph{lib/oppress},
\emph{loy/betray}, \emph{auth/subv}, \emph{sanc/degrade}), and examine
how the resulting directions relate to each other in representation
space. The directions neither collapse into a single moral detector nor
isolate from one another. They span five effective dimensions, the
ceiling for six mean-centered directions, which rules out collapse, and
they share a positive common component, which is the
\textbf{signature of integration}. That shared component (mean pairwise
cosine 0.26) is ${\sim}20\times$ a matched non-moral concept battery
built identically (0.013; paired $\Delta = 0.223$, CI [0.202, 0.244],
excluding 0), so the integration is moral-specific relative to that
battery; whether it is specifically moral rather than generic affective
salience is the open residual.

The geometry is consistent across architectures and scale and reaches
its integration regime early in pre-training, well before probe
accuracy saturates, then differentiates slowly. The structure the model
discovers shows \textbf{no evidence} of the individualizing/binding
distinction predicted by Moral Foundations Theory, though that test is
underpowered (20 partitions, smallest achievable $p = 0.05$), so we
report no evidence rather than absence; what structure it does form is
grounded in corpus statistics rather than human theoretical categories.

Extending to moral dilemmas, each dilemma direction composes only
partially from its component foundations: ${\sim}12$\% of its variance
projects onto them (about $2.7\times$ a mismatched-pair baseline), and
the remaining ${\sim}88$\% encodes conflict-specific structure, with
near-balanced loading on the two foundations in tension. The model represents
moral tension itself, not a pre-resolved judgment.
\end{abstract}
