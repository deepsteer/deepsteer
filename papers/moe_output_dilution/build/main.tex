\begin{abstract}
Linear probes can decode moral content from every expert in a
Mixture-of-Experts model --- all 1,024 expert--layer combinations in
OLMoE-1B-7B exceed 75\% accuracy, and the peak layer exceeds 90\%
for all 64 experts. By this standard metric, moral reasoning is
robustly encoded. Yet OLMoE is 3.6$\times$ more fragile than a dense
model of matched size and accuracy: the activation-noise level that
collapses probe performance is $\sigma^* = 1.27$, versus 4.56 for
OLMo-2~1B.

We trace this gap to \emph{output dilution}. The MoE block aggregates
a top-8 weighted average of 64 expert outputs before adding to the
residual stream. The resulting feedforward contribution is
77$\times$ smaller in scale than the dense MLP's, measured as the
standard deviation of the output across inputs. Moral information
survives aggregation intact --- but at a signal level trivially
overwhelmed by noise. Component perturbation confirms that routing
itself is robust ($\sigma^* = 9.3$); the fragility originates
entirely in the diluted output.

A checkpoint trajectory across 11 OLMoE snapshots (step 5K--1.2M)
shows that expert specialization never emerges: Gini coefficients
stay between 0.011 and 0.015 from the earliest checkpoint, while
accuracy rises steadily. The redundancy is architectural, not a
training artifact.

\textbf{For MoE models, redundant encoding is not robust encoding ---
aggregation dilutes the signal that every expert individually
preserves.}
\end{abstract}
