\begin{abstract}
A model can state that an action is wrong and take it anyway. We measure that gap directly,
scenario by scenario, on an open 7B chat model, with the model's own stated judgment as the
reference and a discrete, logged action as the outcome. The panel is pre-registered: 248
scenarios across five pressure families (task completion, sycophancy under pushback, prohibited
shortcuts, in-group favor, third-party harm), each written in a matched agent frame and a
third-person judgment frame, each with a pressure-removed twin and three paraphrased judgment
frames, half written by each of two generator models and cross-labeled by the other.

On OLMo-3-7B-Instruct the model takes the action it judged wrong on one screened scenario in
five by majority vote (0.19, 95\% CI 0.13 to 0.28) and on two rollouts in five. The
measurement sits inside a calibration ladder: it exceeds the same statistic on pressure-removed
twins by 0.10 (0.02 to 0.18), and a known-gap control in which the system prompt orders the
violating action reaches 0.58. The gap survives a paraphrase-majority judgment reference. On the
majority readout it is not resolved against the strictest reference, where all four judgment
frames must agree; on a pre-registered secondary readout that uses the saved next-token
probabilities instead of majority votes, the excess over the null holds at every strictness
level and is largest at the strictest (0.08, 0.03 to 0.13).

The gap has no family structure at this power: third-party harm is not the family where it is
smallest, so the action channel is not organized by the harm content that refusal reads. The
same gap appears in the base model's raw completion frame at least as strongly as in the
instruct model's, on the same scenarios, so post-training does not install it. One family
depends on which model wrote its scenarios; a blind human read finds no construction asymmetry,
and the difference is graded rather than reversed. We release the panel, the harness, the
pre-registration with its sixteen dated amendments, and the per-rollout arrays.
\end{abstract}
