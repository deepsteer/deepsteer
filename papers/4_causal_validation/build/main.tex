\begin{abstract}
Companion paper to \citet{reblitzrichardson2026geometry}, which
identified structured moral representations in language models
via linear probing but acknowledged that probe directions are
correlational.  We provide three forms of validation that these
directions are genuine features of the model's computation.
\textbf{Causal validation:} ablating a foundation's direction from
the residual stream at layer~12 specifically reduces log-probability
of that foundation's continuations (mean specificity $-0.93$
nats), while injecting directions at low amplitude ($\alpha = 1$)
produces specific boosts that saturate into non-specific
degradation at high amplitude --- a dose--response curve consistent
with real feature manipulation rather than noise injection.
\textbf{Behavioral grounding:} projection-based 6-way foundation
classification achieves 67.1\% on held-out test pairs and 85.4\%
on causal evaluation prompts (chance = 16.7\%), but only 26.7\%
on Moral Foundations Vignettes, revealing that real-world moral
stimuli co-activate the care/harm direction regardless of their
target foundation.
\textbf{Sparse autoencoder analysis:} top-100 morally selective
SAE features span a subspace that captures 19.6\% of probe
direction variance ($4.01\times$ random baseline, $p < 0.001$),
indicating partial but significant convergence between
unsupervised feature discovery and supervised direction extraction.
Together, these results establish moral probe directions as
causally load-bearing, behaviorally predictive features suitable
for representation engineering interventions.
\end{abstract}
